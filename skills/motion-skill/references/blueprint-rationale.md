# Video Blueprint: what the evidence says (rationale for blueprint.md)

Evidence check done 2026-10-19. Levels: **strong** means meta-analyses or replicated experiments, **moderate** means some experiments or indirect support, **convention** means practitioner or YouTube guidance only.

1. **Cold open (hook, 15 s or less): convention.** YouTube's own playbook says to grab attention, state the topic early and tease what is coming [7]. No learning-science study tests hooks for learning. Brame's guiding-questions point (state the goal up front) is indirect support [1].
2. **Channel sting (4–6 s): convention, mildly contra-indicated.** YouTube says "a flashy intro ... is not the star" [7], and the coherence/weeding principle says to cut extraneous material [1]. Keep it short or skip it.
3. **Introduction (who/what/promise): moderate.** Telling viewers the learning objective up front improved test scores [1]. The "who" part is convention.
4. **Quick overview (one-sentence thesis + zoomed-out diagram): moderate.** Ausubel's advance organizers: effects are generally positive but have varied across reviews [3][4]. Pre-training (introduce key terms and parts first) is supported in 13 of 16 tests, median d = 0.75 [5].
5. **Roadmap (3–7 numbered topics) + "you are here" tracker: moderate to strong.** This is signaling: a meta-analysis found d ≈ 0.38 [6], and Brame recommends it [1]. The 3–7 range is a convention, not a tested number.
6. **Topic blocks (one concept each, with a bridge-in and bridge-out): strong.** Segmenting is supported in 10 of 10 tests, median d = 0.79 [5], and by a 56-study meta-analysis showing gains in retention and transfer, including when the system sets the pace [2]. Bridges are signaling [6]. The worked example and misconception slots fit the coherence principle but were not checked here.
7. **Recap line (12 words or fewer): moderate as a summary, weak as retrieval.** A concise visual-plus-verbal summary beat longer versions [8]. A recap the narrator states is not retrieval practice. Only a pause-and-recall prompt counts, and that reduced mind wandering and improved learning in a 21-minute lecture [9]. Spacing recaps within 5 minutes is convention.
8. **Full picture (overview rebuilt): moderate.** It works as a concise summary [8] and closes the advance organizer [3][4]. No study tests "rebuild the overview" as such.
9. **Takeaways + next step, outro: convention.** These follow YouTube platform mechanics (end screens [11]). For learning, a takeaway prompt only helps if it makes viewers recall rather than reread [9].

Format note: Guo et al. found engagement near 100% for videos under 6 minutes [1]. That supports the 5-minute target.

## YouTube rules (help center, checked 2026-10-19)

- **Chapters** [10]: the first timestamp must be 00:00. List at least 3 timestamps in ascending order. Each chapter must be at least 10 seconds long.
- **End screens** [11]:
  - Placement: the last 5–20 seconds of the video, and the video must be at least 25 seconds long.
  - Elements: up to 4 on a 16:9 video (other aspect ratios may allow fewer).
  - Element types: video, playlist, subscribe, channel, external link (YPP members only), and templates.
  - Custom image: at least 300 × 300 px recommended.
  - Layout: Studio offers a grid, snap-to-grid and snap-to-element. The help center gives **no** official safe-area margins or element pixel sizes. The commonly quoted ~615×345 px video tiles, ~294×294 px circles and squares, and 10% edge margins come from third-party sources only.
  - Behavior: cards and watermarks are hidden while the end screen shows. End screens do not appear on made-for-kids videos, 360 videos, mobile web (except iPad), YouTube Music or YouTube Kids.
  - For the outro, plan about 20 s with clean space for 2–4 elements.
